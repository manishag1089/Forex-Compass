How the law is layered

One statute, five layers of instruments

FEMA advice usually goes wrong not because the law is unknown, but because the wrong layer is quoted. Master Directions instruct AD banks — the actual entitlement often sits one or two layers up, in Rules made by the Central Government or Regulations made by RBI.

FEMA Act, 1999

The parent statute. Defines capital vs current account transactions and delegates rule/regulation-making power.

NDI Rules 2019 (FDI) · OI Rules 2022 (ODI)

Made by the Ministry of Finance under Sec. 46 — this is where entry routes, sectoral caps and the round-tripping layering test actually live.

OI Regulations 2022 · Borrowing & Lending Regs 2018 · NDI Reporting Regs 2019

Made by RBI under Sec. 47, gazetted with a FEMA notification number (e.g. FEMA 400/2022-RB).

~14 FED Master Directions

Consolidated operating instructions to Authorised Dealer banks — one per topic (LRS, Overseas Investment, ECB, Foreign Investment, Reporting, etc.), each carrying an "updated as on" date.

A.P. (DIR Series) Circulars · FEMA Notifications · DPIIT Press Notes

The actual amendment mechanism. If a Master Direction feels stale, this is the layer to check first.

Tier 4 — RBI FED Master Directions

The core forex Master Directions

Originally consolidated 1 January 2016 from years of A.P. (DIR Series) circulars, and re-issued whenever amended. The badge shows the dominant compliance route for that topic — many directions mix routes by transaction type.

Governing Rules & Regulations behind the Directions

FED MD 7/2015-16 · Individuals only

Liberalised Remittance Scheme

The only outward-remittance facility available to resident individuals as such — not to corporates, partnership firms, HUFs (for most purposes) or trusts.

Automatic
within
limit

The headline number

USD 250,000 per Financial Year, per resident individual, cumulative across all AD banks and across both current- and capital-account uses combined. Remittance beyond this needs specific prior RBI permission. Confirm this figure is still current — it is periodically revised by RBI circular.

Permissible current-account uses

Private visits · gifts and donations · going abroad for employment or emigration · maintenance of close relatives abroad · business trips · medical treatment abroad · studies abroad (including self-funded).

Permissible capital-account uses

Opening and maintaining a foreign currency account abroad · purchase of immovable property abroad · Overseas Portfolio Investment (OPI) in listed foreign shares/debt · extending loans to NRI relatives · funding an overseas subsidiary's equity as part of an ODI structure (subject to the ODI framework applying to that portion).

Prohibited

Prohibited uses

Margin trading and lottery/betting remittances · purchase of Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds in the secondary market · remittance to FATF non-cooperative jurisdictions · trading in foreign exchange abroad · purchase of any item specifically restricted under the current Foreign Trade Policy.

Documentation

Form A2PAN mandatoryPurpose code requiredAD-bank due diligence

Tax Collected at Source (TCS) applies under the Income-Tax Act above prescribed thresholds, with a lower rate typically for education funded through an education loan — verify the current TCS slab, since Finance Act amendments change these rates most years.

OI Rules/Regulations/Directions 2022 · FED MD 15/2024-25

Overseas Direct Investment

Governs an Indian Party's "financial commitment" — equity, debt, or guarantees — to or on behalf of a Foreign Entity. Effective from the 22 August 2022 overhaul that replaced the old ODI Regulations.

Automatic
route

Automatic route ceiling

Financial commitment up to 400% of the Indian Party's net worth (per its last audited balance sheet) is generally permitted without prior approval, subject to no adverse report against the Indian Party and other standard conditions. This cap and the definition of "financial commitment" are exactly the kind of figure to re-verify against the live Master Direction — it has moved before.

Approval
route

When prior approval is needed instead

Commitment exceeding the automatic-route ceiling · investment into countries/sectors specifically restricted by RBI · the Foreign Entity is engaged in real estate or banking business (beyond permitted exceptions) · the structure fails the round-tripping layering test below.

The round-tripping / layering test — Rule 19(3)

An Indian Party cannot make a financial commitment to a Foreign Entity that has already invested, or is investing, into India if doing so creates a structure with more than two layers of subsidiaries counted from the Indian Party. Within two layers, round-trip structures sit on the automatic route; beyond that, prior RBI approval is required.

Documentation & ongoing compliance

Form FC → generates UINAPR due 31 DecemberDisinvestment report within 30 days

Form FC is filed through the Authorised Dealer (via the RBI OID application) before or at the time of the first financial commitment, generating a Unique Identification Number (UIN) for that Foreign Entity. An Annual Performance Report is then due every 31 December for as long as the entity exists — including when it is dormant or under liquidation — until formal closure is reported. A missed APR is the single most common trigger for compounding proceedings and blocks fresh ODI until regularised.

NDI Rules 2019 · FED MD 11/2017-18

Foreign Investment in India

Inbound equity investment is governed by the Non-Debt Instruments Rules 2019 (a Central Government rule, not an RBI regulation) — this is why FDI policy changes (Press Notes) come from DPIIT/Ministry of Commerce, not only from RBI.

Automatic
route

Automatic vs Government route

Most sectors sit on the automatic route (no prior approval, only post-facto reporting). Government-route approval is required for specified sectors (defence beyond caps, certain media, multi-brand retail nuances) and — critically — for any investment where the beneficial owner is situated in, or is a citizen of, a country sharing a land border with India (Press Note 3 of 2020), regardless of sector or amount.

What counts as "FDI" — capital instruments

Equity shares, fully and mandatorily convertible debentures, and fully and mandatorily convertible preference shares. Anything optionally convertible or non-convertible is treated as debt — it falls outside the NDI Rules and typically into the ECB framework instead.

Pricing guidelines

Issue or transfer price must not be less than fair value on a resident-to-non-resident transaction, and not more than fair value on a non-resident-to-resident transaction, determined under an internationally accepted pricing methodology by a SEBI-registered merchant banker or a practising Chartered Accountant.

Documentation — filed via the FIRMS portal

FC-GPR — 30 daysFC-TRS — 60 daysDI (downstream)FLA — by 15 July

The Entity Master Form (EMF) must be current before any transaction form is filed. FC-GPR reports a fresh issue of capital instruments to a non-resident within 30 days of allotment; FC-TRS reports a transfer of capital instruments between a resident and non-resident within 60 days. An Indian company that is itself owned/controlled by non-residents and invests further into another Indian company must report that as a downstream investment (Form DI), applying the same sectoral conditions all over again.

FEM (Borrowing & Lending) Regs 2018 · FED MD 5/2018-19

External Commercial Borrowings

Two tracks — Foreign Currency denominated ECB and INR-denominated ECB — each with its own eligible-borrower list, recognised-lender list, minimum maturity and end-use restrictions.

Minimum Average Maturity & cost ceiling

Typically 3 years minimum average maturity, shortened to around 1 year for manufacturing-sector borrowers up to a prescribed threshold, and generally 5 years where the lender is a foreign equity holder and proceeds fund working capital, general corporate purposes, or repayment of INR loans. All-in-cost is capped at a benchmark rate (now SOFR-based, post-LIBOR transition) plus a prescribed spread. Both the maturity table and the spread are revised periodically — verify against the current Master Direction before quoting a figure to a client.

Negative
list

End-use restrictions

ECB proceeds generally cannot fund real estate activity (barring specific exceptions), pure working capital or general corporate purposes outside the specific permitted track/tenure, on-lending for such restricted purposes, or repayment of existing rupee loans except where explicitly permitted.

Documentation

Form ECB → LRNForm ECB-2 monthlyFiled via CIMS7 working days of month-end

Form ECB is filed through the Authorised Dealer bank before drawdown, generating a Loan Registration Number (LRN) — drawdown cannot happen without it. Thereafter, a monthly Form ECB-2 return is due within 7 working days of month-end, now filed on RBI's CIMS platform. Late filing of either attracts a Late Submission Fee, and any change in terms requires a revised Form ECB.
